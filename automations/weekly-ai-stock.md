# Weekly AI stock opportunities (scheduled, unattended)

This runs headlessly on Saturdays at 09:00 Pacific. Nobody is watching the session, so never wait for input. Prior reports are in `reports/` in the current working directory (files ending in `-codex.md` came from the earlier Codex version of this task). Save this week's report as `reports/YYYY-MM-DD.md`, then deliver it by sending it with the Gmail `send_message` tool to barryyao1017@gmail.com, subject "Weekly AI stock opportunities — YYYY-MM-DD". Robinhood market-data tools may be used for quotes and fundamentals; never use any trading, order, alert or watchlist tool.

Produce my weekly AI stock opportunity report using fresh research and current timestamped market data. Use research subagents to cover independent sector groups, then critically compare and verify their findings. Research only; never place trades.

Investment mandate: prioritize AI buildout suppliers benefiting from massive, visible demand at attractive valuations, not growth at any price. Add zero to two long-term AI beneficiaries only when compelling opportunities arise, within the total count. The buildout cycle can justify multiyear holdings. Classify each investment thesis rather than permanently classifying tickers. Distinguish proven cash-generating suppliers from speculative projects dependent on financing, approvals or commercialization.

Screen all of these verticals each week, deepening research on promising candidates: power generation including nuclear/gas/renewables/geothermal; utilities/grids; electrical equipment; backup power/storage; cooling/water; data center real estate; engineering/construction; materials; AI/general/edge processors; memory; foundries; semiconductor equipment; packaging/testing; chip design tools/IP; servers; networking/optics; storage; sensors; hyperscale and specialized/neo/sovereign clouds; edge infrastructure; foundation models; inference/model deployment; AI developer tools; data platforms/integration/governance/training data; agent infrastructure. Long-term coverage includes cybersecurity; developer and IT operations software; productivity/workflow/customer service/sales/marketing/finance/HR/legal/design/engineering software; retail/e-commerce; advertising/search/social; media/gaming; healthcare/devices/biotech/pharma; banking/payments/insurance/capital markets; manufacturing; automotive/logistics; aerospace/defense; agriculture; energy; telecom; construction/real estate; education; travel; government suppliers; professional services; robotics/autonomous systems/drones/consumer AI devices/spatial computing. Do not confine discovery to companies previously mentioned. Verify public investability; private firms are competitive context only.

Target 15 distinct ideas: five large caps (USD market cap >=10B), five mid caps (2B to <10B), five small caps (300M to <2B), using current market caps. Default to US-listed stocks and ADRs, including foreign issuers. Do not silently relabel sizes or pad recommendations to meet quotas. If qualified buys are scarce, fill with clearly labeled watchlist candidates and valuation-based entry conditions; disclose any remaining shortfall. Balance sectors where merits support it; size diversification alone does not eliminate shared AI-cycle risk.

For each candidate verify demand/orders and contract quality, revenue and earnings trajectory, cash conversion, competitive position, customer concentration, capex, debt, dilution, cyclicality, and whether valuation already discounts the upside. Use latest filings, earnings releases, guidance and credible current market data, with direct source links. Separate reported facts, management guidance, consensus estimates, and your assumptions. Use sector-appropriate valuation and normalized earnings for cyclicals. Calculate entry ranges and bear/base/bull price/return scenarios from explicit earnings/cash-flow and valuation assumptions; do not invent precision or rely solely on analyst targets. Show investment horizon and annualized returns for comparisons across horizons. If a critical price or financial input is unavailable or stale, disclose it and withhold an actionable buy designation.

Keep the delivered report very short despite extensive research: about 1000–1400 words, three size sections with five compact entries each, and a brief market/changes summary. Each entry: company/ticker; buildout or long-term; New/Repeat/Changed; Buy-range or Watchlist; price and actual market cap with as-of date; concise thesis and why included THIS WEEK; valuation-backed entry range; bear/base/bull returns and horizon; key risk/thesis invalidation; evidence links. Include two distinct gauges: investment aggressiveness 1–5 (1 established economics/limited speculative assumptions; 3 material cycle/execution dependence; 5 speculative financing/commercialization assumptions), and share-price volatility Low/Medium/High/Very high supported by observed volatility/drawdowns when available, with estimates labeled. Smaller size does not automatically imply better upside. Mention the main scenario assumptions compactly.

Compare with prior reports in `reports/`. Repeats are allowed because I may not have purchased prior ideas, but explain why each remains attractive; actively search for alternatives. Flag new entrants, exits, entry-range crossings and weakened theses. Do not assume recommendations are holdings. For the first run establish the baseline. Deliver a report each week as explicitly requested.
